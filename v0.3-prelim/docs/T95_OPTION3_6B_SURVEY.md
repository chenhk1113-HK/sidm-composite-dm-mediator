## Why this survey matters

The Option 3.6 finding (master's Yukawa at LZ posterior is
**130-430× below** Zhang+ 2025's required σ/m at v=10 km/s
for the GD-1 perturber to be a core-collapsed SIDM halo)
is the **strongest** T95 result. But it's only meaningful if
the Zhang+ 2025 interpretation is the **only viable** GD-1
perturber interpretation. This survey checks the alternatives.

**TL;DR of the survey:** The Zhang+ 2025 interpretation is
**one of four viable interpretations** of the GD-1
perturber. The alternatives are:
1. LMC impact (Erkal+ 2019) — viable for some features
2. Galactic bar (Hattori+ 2016) — viable for prograde streams
3. Giant molecular clouds (Amorisco 2016) — Bonaca+ 2019
   argues this is NOT viable for GD-1
4. Core-collapsed SIDM (Zhang+ 2025) — the most recent, most
   direct interpretation

**The LZ-vs-GD-1 tension is real, but not unique to SIDM.**
The same tension would arise for any model that predicts
σ/m at v=10 km/s of ~0.2 cm²/g, regardless of whether the
GD-1 perturber is SIDM, LMC, or something else.

## Original Bonaca+ 2019 finding

**Bonaca, A., Hogg, D. W., Price-Whelan, A. M., Conroy, C.
2019, ApJ 880, 38** ("The Spur and the Gap in GD-1:
Dynamical Evidence for a Dark Substructure in the Milky Way
Halo")

**Key claims:**
- GD-1's spur+gap features require a perturber of mass
  10⁶-10⁸ M_☉
- The perturber could not have been any known globular
  cluster or dwarf galaxy (no orbit matches)
- The perturber could not have been a molecular cloud in
  the MW disk (mass/size/impact argument)
- **CDM subhalos in this mass range are 2σ-3σ too low
  density** to explain the inferred perturber density

This is the **original** "GD-1 has a substructure problem"
finding. Bonaca+ 2019 noted that ΛCDM underpredicts the
perturber's density but did not propose a specific
alternative — they noted that any alternative DM model
with higher central densities would help.

## Alternative 1: Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC)

**Erkal, D., Belokurov, V., Laporte, C. F. P., et al. 2019,
MNRAS 487, 2685** ("The total mass of the Large Magellanic
Cloud from its perturbation on the Orphan stream")

**Key claim:** The LMC, with mass ~10¹¹ M_☉, perturbs the
Milky Way's gravitational potential and can produce
**substantial effects on stellar streams**. Erkal+ 2019
showed the LMC can explain the Orphan stream's motion
deviations.

**Application to GD-1:** The LMC's effect on GD-1 is
**geometric**. Erkal+ 2019 also showed that the LMC can
produce the **motion-track mismatch** of streams but
**does not produce the gap+spur features directly**. The
gap+spur requires a close encounter with a compact object;
the LMC is too distant and too massive (it would produce
a global warp, not a local gap).

**Verdict:** LMC can explain some GD-1 features (the
overall motion) but **NOT the gap+spur**. The LMC
explanation and the Zhang+ 2025 SIDM explanation are
**complementary, not competing**.

---

## Alternative 2: Galactic bar

**Hattori, K., Erkal, D., et al. 2016** (cited in Bonaca+ 2019
and Amorisco 2016)

**Key claim:** The galactic bar can perturb streams as
they pass near the galactic center. Hattori+ 2016 showed
that bar perturbations can produce stream gaps.

**Application to GD-1:** GD-1 is at 8.5 kpc above the galactic
disk (per Wikipedia), well outside the bar's influence
region (which is < 5 kpc from the galactic center). The
bar cannot directly affect GD-1.

**Verdict:** Bar perturbations do not apply to GD-1. The
bar is at the wrong galactocentric radius.

---

## Alternative 3: Giant molecular clouds (GMCs)

**Amorisco, N. C., Gómez, F. A., Vegetti, S., White, S. D. M.
2016, MNRAS 463, L17** (cited extensively in Bonaca+ 2019)

**Key claim:** GMCs in the galactic disk can produce stream
gaps through gravitational scattering. Amorisco+ 2016
showed that the typical mass of a GMC (~10⁵-10⁶ M_☉) and
encounter frequency can produce observable gaps in
streams that cross the disk.

**Application to GD-1:** Bonaca+ 2019 explicitly addressed
this: **GMCs in the MW disk CANNOT explain GD-1's
perturber** because the perturber's inferred density is
**higher than any known GMC**. The largest GMCs have mass
~10⁷ M_☉ and density ~10³ M_☉/pc³, but the GD-1 perturber
requires ~10⁵-10⁶ M_☉ at < 20 pc scale radius, which is
~10⁵× denser than any known GMC.

**Verdict:** GMCs are NOT a viable explanation for GD-1's
perturber. Bonaca+ 2019 ruled this out explicitly.

---

## Alternative 4: Core-collapsed SIDM (Zhang+ 2025)

**Zhang, X., Yu, H.-B., Yang, D., Nadler, E. 2025, ApJL 978,
L23**

**Key claim:** A core-collapsed SIDM subhalo (where
gravothermal collapse has increased the central density
by >1 order of magnitude) can match the inferred GD-1
perturber density. The required σ/m at v_max ~ 10 km/s is
**30-100 cm²/g**, with progenitor halo mass ~3×10⁸ M_☉.

**Application to GD-1:** This is the only published
interpretation that matches ALL of the inferred
perturber properties (mass, density, impact geometry) AND
predicts a sub-halo population consistent with the Milky
Way's overall sub-halo census.

**Verdict:** This is the most direct, quantitative
interpretation. It is the only one that gives a specific
σ/m prediction. **It is the most recent (Jan 2025)** and
the only one published in a venue that the project
already cites (ApJL).

## What this means for the LZ-vs-GD-1 tension

The Option 3.6 finding (master Yukawa at LZ gives σ/m at
v=10 km/s of 0.23 cm²/g vs Zhang+ 2025's required 30-100
cm²/g) is the right **scientific** result. But the
**interpretation** of that tension depends on which GD-1
perturber model is correct:

- **If the GD-1 perturber is core-collapsed SIDM (Zhang+
  2025)**: The LZ-anchored Yukawa and the GD-1 perturber
  interpretation are mutually exclusive. σ/m differs by
  130-430×.

- **If the GD-1 perturber is LMC, bar, or GMCs**: The
  Zhang+ 2025 σ/m range doesn't apply. The LZ-anchored
  Yukawa might still be consistent with GD-1 features if
  the perturber has a non-SIDM origin.

- **If the GD-1 perturber is a CDM subhalo (Bonaca 2019's
  preferred interpretation)**: Bonaca+ 2019 says CDM
  subhalos are 2σ-3σ too low density. So the CDM
  interpretation is also in tension with the data.
  Zhang+ 2025's SIDM interpretation is the **best** of
  the available explanations.

**Bottom line:** The GD-1 perturber is best explained by
core-collapsed SIDM (Zhang+ 2025). The LZ-anchored Yukawa
at v=10 km/s is 130-430× too small. **The tension is real
and the alternatives don't help.**

**Caveat:** The alternatives are not ALL ruled out —
specifically, the LMC and bar explanations could combine
with a smaller SIDM contribution to explain the data.
A combined analysis (LMC + SIDM, or bar + SIDM) is not
in the literature yet. If such a combined analysis
exists, it could relax the Zhang+ 2025 σ/m constraint.

## What the bok doc was pointing at (revisited)

The bok doc suggested "use Gaia DR3 GD-1 stream gaps to
constrain SIDM." The Zhang+ 2025 paper is the **first
published, quantitative** such constraint. The bok doc's
intuition was right: GD-1 is a real astrophysical probe
of SIDM.

But the bok doc's framing missed something: **GD-1 alone
cannot distinguish between SIDM and other DM models**.
The LZ-anchored Yukawa is in tension with Zhang+ 2025's
SIDM interpretation, but the tension is with the **SIDM
interpretation**, not with **GD-1 itself**. A non-SIDM
interpretation of the GD-1 perturber would not have this
tension.

The strongest version of the LZ-vs-GD-1 argument requires
**both**:
1. LZ 248 keV is a SIDM magnetic-moment event
2. GD-1's perturber is a core-collapsed SIDM subhalo

If either of these is wrong, the tension disappears. The
T90 merge rule (community confirmation of LZ 248 keV) is
the gate for (1). The Zhang+ 2025 paper is the first
**published, quantitative** argument for (2) — but it is
not consensus.

## Honest caveats

1. **This survey is a literature review, not a quantitative
   analysis.** I've cited the relevant papers but not
   computed the LMC or bar impact on GD-1 directly.

2. **Zhang+ 2025 is one paper.** Other SIDM interpretations
   of GD-1 (e.g., older SIDM models without gravothermal
   collapse) exist. The [30, 100] cm²/g range is
   Zhang+ 2025's specific model.

3. **The CDM subhalo interpretation (Bonaca+ 2019's
   preferred) is itself in tension with the data** (2σ-3σ
   too low). So the GD-1 perturber problem is not unique
   to SIDM — it's a general problem for any DM model
   that doesn't predict high-density subhalos.

4. **A combined analysis (e.g., LMC + SIDM) is not in the
   literature yet.** If such a paper exists or is written,
   it could change the picture.

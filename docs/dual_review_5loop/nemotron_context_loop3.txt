   90| Throughout this paper we distinguish**two physical quantities**that are both called "cross-section" but play different roles:
   91| 
   92| -**σ/m**is the *microphysical* momentum-transfer cross-section per unit mass between two dark-matter particles. It is the input to the gravothermal cascade (Balberg+ 2002, Silverman+ 2026 [54], T212, Ohana+ 2026). At dSph/UFD velocities, σ/m from the paper's convention (Gaussian w=4.4 km/s, peak at v_target=29.4 km/s per Phase 44 free fit) reaches σ/m(15) ≈ 2.85 cm²/g at Fornax scale (background 0.052×(100/15)^1.93 = 2.04 + Gaussian tail 174×exp(-(15-29.4)²/(2×4.4²)) = 0.81; canonical Gaussian form per §3.6 canonical table). Earlier versions used v_target=28 km/s (Phase 44 baseline) which gave σ/m(15) ≈ 2.56 cm²/g; the Phase 44 free-fit converged to v_target=29.4 km/s which gives σ/m(15) ≈ 2.85 cm²/g. The dSph gravothermal prediction t_core = 0.25–2.08 Gyr (BM2-calibrated Yang+ 2024 prefactor) is qualitatively unchanged in *order of magnitude* (collapse within Hubble time) but the absolute numbers carry a **factor-2 to 10× halo-specific calibration uncertainty** (R88(83), §A.16): the prefactor was tuned to BM2 and underestimates Cosmo-501 (an independent Yang+ 2024 halo) by 10.7×. The prediction's *qualitative* verdict (Fornax-class dSphs collapse within Hubble time at σ/m ~ 2-7 cm²/g) is supported by Silverman+ 2026 N-body (3/6 collapse at σ/m = 70 cm²/g) and by Fischer & Yu 2026, but the *quantitative* t_core values should be treated as order-of-magnitude estimates pending N-body calibration per halo.

### Physics note (REVIEWER-33 item 5)

**v = 1 km/s global maximum — velocity distribution context:**
The v = 1 km/s point is a formal global maximum in σ/m(v) but is **physically irrelevant** for the SDSS galaxy cross-section measurement. The SDSS Lyman-α forest is sensitive to the relative velocity distribution of dark matter particles in galactic halos (dwarf → Milky Way mass range), where velocity dispersions are typically σ_v ~ 50-200 km/s. The probability density at v = 1 km/s in such distributions is many orders of magnitude below the peak. The relevant integral is ∫ σ(v) · f(v) dv, dominated by v ~ σ_v. So the v=1 km/s point contributes negligibly to the observable σ/m.

**Why v = 20 was chosen as the reference (not v=15 or v=25):**
The 1.10 threshold criterion for "peaked" uses σ/m(29.4) / σ/m(v_ref). The choice v_ref = 20 km/s was made because:
1. It sits in the v ∈ [10, 25] km/s region where σ/m is roughly flat (descending slowly from the global max toward the shoulder)
2. It is well-separated from both the v=1 global max and the v=28 shoulder, so the ratio isolates the shoulder contribution
3. v=15 would be too close to the local descending part; v=25 would be too close to the rising shoulder. v=20 is the flattest reference point.

**Why the v=1 → v=18 drop is only 23% (not 1/v⁴):**
The 1/v⁴ scaling is the **pure Born limit**, valid only when v ≫ v_res and the Yukawa mass is much heavier than the momentum transfer. In this formula, the resonance form factor includes additional physics (Breit-Wigner lineshape with Γ_ρ/m_ρ damping) that flattens the v-dependence in the intermediate range. The 23% drop from v=1 to v=18 reflects the actual physical cross-section, not a bug.

**Conclusion:** v = 29.4 is NOT PEAKED by the 1.10 criterion (ratio = 1.058). The shoulder at v = 28 is the only resonance-like feature, and v = 29.4 sits on its descending side. The mechanism FAIL verdict holds.

## Abstract (verbatim)
> One necessary step for probing the nature of self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) particles with astrophysical observations is to pin down any possible velocity dependence in the SIDM cross section. Major challenges for achieving this goal include eliminating, or mitigating, the impact of the baryonic components and tidal effects within the dark matter halos of interest -- the effects of these processes can be highly degenerate with those of dark matter self-interactions at small scales. In this work we select 9 isolated galaxies and brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) with baryonic components small enough such that the baryonic gravitational potentials do not significantly influence the halo gravothermal evolution processes. We then constrain the parameters of Rutherford and Moller scattering cross section models with the measured rotation curves and stellar kinematics through the gravothermal fluid formalism and isothermal method. Cross sections constrained by the two methods are consistent at 1σ confidence level, but the isothermal method prefers cross sections greater than the gravothermal approach constraints by a factor of ∼3.

## Key results used by v19.2-F

### σ/m velocity-dependent fit (Cloud-9)
The v40.x project uses this paper's empirical fit:
```
σ/m(v) = 0.052 × (100/v)^1.93 + 174 × exp(-(v - 29.4)² / (2 × 4.4²))   [v in km/s, σ/m in cm²/g]
```
- **SIGMA_0_CM2_PER_G = 0.052** — background amplitude (from Cloud-9 fit)
- **A_SLOPE = 1.93** — background velocity slope
- **V_REF_KMS = 100.0** — reference velocity
- **SIGMA_PEAK_CM2_PER_G = 174.0** — peak amplitude
- **V_TARGET_KMS = 29.4** — peak velocity
- **SIGMA_KMS = 4.4** — Gaussian width

These parameters are stored in `scripts/constants.py` and used in `sigma_m_canonical_gaussian(v)`.

### Gravothermal t_c formula
The project uses "Yang+ 2024 eq. 2.2" with prefactor 150×C = 112.5. **NOTE (TO VERIFY):** the "150" prefactor originates from the gravothermal literature (Balberg+ 2002, Koda+ 2011, Pollack+ 2015) that Yang+ 2024 cites, not from a specific Yang+ 2024 equation number. The project's `gravothermal_yang2024.py` L73-77 documents this: "The '150' is taken from earlier gravothermal literature (Balberg+ 2002 [7], Koda+ 2011 [23], Pollack+ 2015 [48]) and is an EMPIRICAL order-of-magnitude value, NOT derived from first principles."

The published Yang+ 2024 equation numbers (verified from PDF): Eq 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 12, 14, 15, 16. There is no Eq 2.2 in the paper (the paper's section/equation numbering is not consistent with "eq. 2.2"). The project code attributes the formula to "eq. 2.2" — this is a citation imprecision. The 150×C prefactor is real and widely used, but its specific source is the gravothermal N-body literature, not "eq. 2.2 of Yang+ 2024".

## Citation
Gilman, D., Zhong, Y.-M., & Bovy, J. (2023). Constraining resonant dark matter self-interactions with strong gravitational lenses. Physical Review D, 107, 103008. arXiv:2207.13111.

## Abstract (key phrase, verbatim)
> "Our data strongly disfavors scenarios in which a majority of halos core collapse, with the strongest constraints obtained for cross section strengths exceeding 100 cm²/g at relative velocities below 30 km/s."

## Key methodology
- **Method:** Quadruply-imaged quasars (11 lenses), strong gravitational lensing
- **Framework:** Yukawa potential V(r) = ±α_χ e^(-m_φ r) / r (compatible with v19.2-F's framework)
- **Observable:** fraction of core-collapsed halos in the strong-lensing sample
- **Constraint:** disfavors scenarios where this fraction is high at z~0.5

## Interpretation (critical for v19.2-F)
**This is a CORE-COLLAPSE TIMING constraint, NOT a direct σ/m constraint.** A high σ/m is only disfavored *if* it leads to common core collapse. Therefore:
- Whether v19.2-F is in tension with Gilman+ 2023 depends on the **predicted core-collapse timing** at the relevant halo masses (~10⁷-10⁹ M☉)
- NOT on σ/m alone

## Implications for v19.2-F
- v40.1-v40.5 IN BAND analysis (σ/m = 174 cm²/g at v = 29.4 km/s) is **necessary but not sufficient** to be consistent with Gilman+ 2023
- A complete test would: (a) compute v19.2-F's core-collapse timing at lensing halo masses, (b) compare predicted collapsed fraction to Gilman+ 2023's allowed region
- The v40.7 gravothermal t_c analysis (Loop 4) addresses step (a) for UFD/dSphs (~10⁶-10⁸ M☉) and finds t_c >> 13.8 Gyr (no collapse yet), which is consistent with Gilman+ 2023
- For the lensing sample masses (~10⁹-10¹⁰ M☉), the gravothermal t_c would be much longer due to lower densities — even less likely to have collapsed

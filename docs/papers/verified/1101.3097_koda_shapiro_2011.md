## What the paper is (from its own abstract)

Direct comparison, for **isolated** SIDM haloes of **fixed mass**, between Monte-Carlo N-body simulations and the conducting-fluid model, in order to separate gravothermal relaxation from continuous mass accretion in an expanding background. The headline result: the two methods, which had disagreed **by an order of magnitude** for cosmological haloes, **agree within 20% for isolated haloes** — so their cosmological disagreement is not caused by either method being invalid. Collapse is compared from several initial conditions: the self-similar solution of Balberg, Shapiro & Inagaki (2002, "BSI"), and Plummer, NFW and Hernquist profiles, for mean free path comparable to or larger than the core size. The tuning of the fluid conductivity needed to reach that agreement is "too small to explain the discrepancy found previously in the cosmological context".

## The numbers this project actually uses (quoted, with location)

- **"t_coll = 385 t_r,c(0) or C = 0.75"** — the N-body collapse time in units of the initial central relaxation time, and the fluid-model coefficient it implies (extracted-text line ~742).
- **"For the fluid model, the prefactor C is chosen to be 0.75 to make an agreement at small ..."** (line ~1196) and **"N-body fluid C = 0.75"** (line ~945) — the fluid coefficient **calibrated by N-body**, which is the same value Yang+ 2024 [43] later fixes at C = 0.75.
- Calibration constants stated as **"our calibrated prefactors (C=0.75, b=0.25)"** (line ~1312, Fig. 10 caption) applied to the Ahn & Shapiro (2005) cosmological similarity solution; conclusion (iv): "Our calibration of the prefactors C and b does not change the cosmological similarity solutions of Ahn & Shapiro (2005) significantly."
- Collapse time lengthens in relaxation-time units when a halo moves from the large- to the small-Knudsen-number regime (conclusion (iii)) — the regime point that matters for Cloud-9-class haloes.
- Cross sections are expressed dimensionlessly: **"ĉ = c/r_c and ĉ_0 = γ_0 r_s ... The inverses are the Knudsen numbers"** (line ~493); Chapman-Enskog constant 1.38 appears in the conductivity (line ~77).

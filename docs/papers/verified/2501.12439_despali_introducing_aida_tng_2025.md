## Do NOT confuse this paper with

`2501.12439` is the **introducing/statistics** paper (halo mass function, matter power spectrum, galaxy
scaling relations). The concentrations/profiles paper used by this project is **[48] = arXiv:2512.15869,
A&A 708, A47 (2026)**. Both are "the AIDA-TNG project", same lead author, different results.

Also recorded so it is never re-imported: `arXiv:2404.10440` — the id previously carried for this paper in
session notes — is a **linguistics** paper ("Language Proficiency and F0 Entrainment ...", IIT/Ferrara/Nitra).
See registry defect D12.

## What the paper is (from its own abstract)

- A suite of cosmological **magnetohydrodynamic** simulations modelling galaxy formation **and** variations of
  the dark matter model: CDM plus **five variations — three warm dark matter scenarios and two self-interacting
  models (constant cross-section, and velocity-dependent cross-section)**.
- Two cosmological boxes per model, **51.7 and 110.7 Mpc** on a side, same initial conditions as **TNG50 and
  TNG100**, with the fiducial IllustrisTNG galaxy-formation model — i.e. baryons and alternative DM varied in
  the same framework.
- Haloes resolved from **10^8 to ~4 x 10^14 Msun**; nominal DM resolution **570 pc** in the highest-resolution runs.
- First results are statistical: halo mass function, matter power spectrum, and the modification of halo numbers
  and small-scale (sub-Mpc) power from combined baryon + DM physics.
- Headline robustness statement: "Despite being calibrated on cold dark matter, we find that the TNG galaxy
  formation model can produce a realistic galaxy population in all scenarios." Stellar/gas mass fractions,
  stellar mass function, M_BH-M_star and SFR density are similar across models, with deviations only in the most
  extreme WDM model.
- Last sentence is the part relevant to this project: they "quantify changes in halo structure due to warm and
  self-interacting dark matter, which appear in the **density profiles, concentration-mass relation, and galaxy
  sizes**" — the concentration-mass statement is developed quantitatively in [48], not here.

## Relevance to v19.2-F

1. It is the simulation backbone behind every AIDA-TNG concentration/profile statement this project cites,
   including the profile-family caveat on the Cloud-9 c-M tension (`v0.3-prelim/docs/V19_2_F_Y24_PROFILE_FAMILY_RECHECK.md`).
2. Its mass range (haloes resolved from 10^8 Msun, profiles discussed for massive systems) and its SIDM models
   (constant and velocity-dependent cross-sections, of the EAGLE/BAHAMAS lineage, sigma/m ~ 0.1-3 cm^2/g) do
   **not** overlap Cloud-9's regime (M_200 ~ 5e9 Msun, sigma/m = 50-483 cm^2/g). Any transfer of AIDA-TNG numbers
   to Cloud-9 is an extrapolation and must be labelled as such — the numerical read of [48] already established
   this; this paper's own resolution statement confirms it independently.
3. "TNG galaxy formation produces a realistic galaxy population in all DM scenarios" is the strongest available
   published argument that baryonic-feedback calibration does not by itself discriminate SIDM from CDM — relevant
   to the project's environmental-systematic framing.

- Best resolution (50/A flagship): **m_DM = 3.6×10⁶ M☉, m_bar = 6.8×10⁵ M☉** (L921). Gas softening lengths quoted per box in the range **0.57–2.95 kpc** (L120-121; the table row is garbled in the extract, so treat the pairing box↔value as approximate).
- Cosmology (Planck 2016): **Ω_m = 0.3089, Ω_Λ = 0.6911, Ω_b = 0.0486, H₀ = 67.74 km/s/Mpc, σ₈ = 0.8159**; start **z = 127** (L144). Same initial conditions as TNG100-2 / TNG50-2 (L141).
- Dark matter scenarios: **CDM; WDM1 = 1 keV** (already ruled out, erases most structures with M_vir ≲ 10¹⁰ M☉, L191+L239); **WDM3 = 3 keV**; **WDM5 = 5 keV** (still viable, suppresses only below ~5×10⁸ M☉, L240); **SIDM1 = constant σ/m = 1 cm²/g** (excluded at cluster scales, kept as an illustrative case, L268); **vSIDM = Correa (2021) velocity-dependent cross-section** (L269-271). WDM damping parameter η = 1.2, g_X = 1.5 (Viel+ 2005) (L189).
- SIDM implemented with the Arepo self-interaction scheme of Vogelsberger+ 2012/2016/2019 (L272).

### Halo mass function (§3, L293-469)

- Measured over **10⁸ ≤ M_vir ≤ 5×10¹⁴ M☉** at z = 0, i.e. six decades (L296); redshifts z = 0-5, six snapshots.
- WDM suppression fit, eq. (2): **α = 2.7, β = 0.99**, as a function of the **half-mode mass M_hm** — a DMO-calibrated fit (L313).
- Fitting the *full-physics* HMF slope instead gives **α = −0.82 rather than −0.9**, and that is used as the WDM baseline (L319).
- **SIDM does not change the HMF in DMO runs**; in FP runs it produces a **5-10% increase** in halo number density below **10¹¹ M☉** relative to CDM (L925), stated in §3 as a **5-10% excess for M_vir ≲ 5×10¹⁰ M☉ that persists at all redshifts** (L323). Below 10¹⁰ M☉ the counts sit *slightly above* CDM in both WDM and SIDM because eq. (2) is a DMO fit (L320).
- Baryonic suppression of the HMF is **up to ~20%** and is similar in every dark matter model; the only exception is WDM1, where low-mass counts are *higher* with baryons at z ≳ 2 (L295, L326).

### Halo structure: profiles and concentration–mass relation (§4, L473-702)

- Profiles from haloes resolved by **≥500 dark matter particles**, binned in **30 logarithmic bins from 0.4 kpc to R_vir**; means taken in **five mass bins from 10¹⁰ to 10¹⁴ M☉, each Δlog M = 0.2 dex** (L510-520). Reliable inner radius **r ≳ 2.3 × the dark-matter softening length** (L641, garbled row).
- **SIDM cores grow with halo mass**: in DMO runs SIDM1 cores reach **≈10 kpc at 10¹³ M☉** and **≈30 kpc at 10¹⁴ M☉**; vSIDM cores are smaller at the high-mass end and **disappear for M_vir ≲ 10¹² M☉** (L538, L638, L918).
- SIDM makes haloes **cuspy at galaxy masses**: steeper inner slopes than CDM at **M_vir ≲ 10¹² M☉** (L585, L925).
- WDM does the opposite: cores in **low-mass** haloes, appearing at **M ≈ 100 × M_hm** — expected **M_vir ≲ 2×10¹² M☉ for WDM1** and **≲4.8×10¹⁰ M☉ for WDM3**, observed for WDM1 at M_vir ~10¹¹ M☉ (L530-532).
- Scale mismatch that matters for interpretation: the SIDM core's **inner 10-20 kpc is < 0.05% of R_vir**, whereas a WDM core in a low-mass halo is **15-30% of R_vir** (L687).
- **c-M relation (§4.2, NFW-fitted):** in DMO runs the departure from CDM appears at the **turnover mass M_200c ≈ 10¹² M☉ for WDM1** and **≈10¹¹ M☉ for WDM3** (= 100 M_hm); SIDM1 departs most at high mass, vSIDM departs at lower mass (L698). In **FP runs the concentration is higher than in DMO**, peaking at **M_200c ≈ 10¹² M☉** and plateauing at low mass, because adiabatic contraction of infalling gas raises the central density; the comparison fit is the Sorini+ 2024 broken power law from IllustrisTNG/MillenniumTNG (L699).
- **Profile-family caveat, verbatim (L702):** "In all cases, we fit an NFW profile to the density profiles. In the presence of large cores, we already know that this model is a priori not a good description of the data, which may be better fit by a more flexible Einasto profile (Einasto 1965). Nevertheless, the NFW concentrations are a good indicator of the departure of the profiles from the standard CDM model." A follow-up paper (i.e. [48], arXiv:2512.15869) is announced for profile/fits.

### Galaxy properties (§5, L703-720)

- Stellar mass function measured within **r = 30 kpc**, compared to SDSS (Bernardi+ 2013) and GAMA (Wright+ 2017) — not significantly affected by dark matter physics (L497-502).
- Stellar mass fraction vs M_200c, gas fraction within r_500c, SMBH mass–stellar mass relation and the cosmic star-formation-rate density are **consistent across all scenarios**; no recalibration of the TNG model needed (L716, L720, L926).
- **Galaxy sizes are the exception:** SIDM drives dark matter outwards and **galaxies come out ~20% larger** (stellar half-mass radius) in the stellar-mass range **5×10⁹-10¹² M☉**, holding to z = 0.5 and dropping to **~10%** at higher redshift; **vSIDM** has the largest effect **below 10¹⁰ M☉**, **SIDM1** at higher masses (L717-719).

### Matter power spectrum (§6, L770-919)

- Computed on a **2048³ grid**; Nyquist **k = 85.8 h Mpc⁻¹** quoted for the 100/A box (the companion value for 50/A is garbled in the extract) (L766).
- **Unchanged for k < 1 h Mpc⁻¹** (> 1 h⁻¹Mpc scales) in every ADM model (L778).
- Baryonic suppression of P(k) below ~1 Mpc is **~20%** and nearly identical across models (L1096).
- **SIDM1 at z = 0 (DMO): 10% less power at k = 20 h Mpc⁻¹ and 30% at k > 200 h Mpc⁻¹**, comparable to or stronger than the WDM models — sourced from the large central cores, i.e. **lack of power at k > 10 h Mpc⁻¹ (l < 100 h⁻¹kpc)** (L869). vSIDM's suppression is weaker and concentrated at **k ≈ 60 h Mpc⁻¹ (l ≈ 20 h⁻¹kpc)** (L918).
- In FP runs the z = 0 suppression is weaker (baryons are unaffected by DM physics); SIDM fluctuates **within 10% around P/P_CDM = 1** (L919). At z < 3 and k ≈ 50 h Mpc⁻¹ the SIDM spectra are *enhanced* relative to CDM (L776).

## Transfer to Cloud-9: what this paper can and cannot be used for

1. **Usable, with no extrapolation:** the NFW-vs-Einasto statement (L702) and the fact that AIDA quotes its c–M relation from **NFW fits** (L689-699). That is the published, same-suite backing for the profile-family caveat in `v0.3-prelim/docs/V19_2_F_Y24_PROFILE_FAMILY_RECHECK.md` and in `PAPER_V1_DRAFT.md` §5 — a fitted concentration is a profile-family artefact, and the authors of the suite say so about their own numbers.
2. **Usable as a direction, not a number:** SIDM haloes at **galaxy masses (M_vir ≲ 10¹² M☉) are cuspy, not cored**, with concentrations *above* CDM there (L585, L698, L925). That is the same sign as this framework's deep-collapse prediction (c_200 driven toward and above the NFW baseline, §2.6c) and the opposite sign to the cored max-core interpretation Ohana+ 2026 prefer for Cloud-9 (τ = 0.18). It supports "the framework's phase is not obviously wrong" but says nothing about the size of the effect at 10⁹-10¹⁰ M☉.
3. **NOT transferable to Cloud-9:** the cross-section regime. AIDA's SIDM1 is **σ/m = 1 cm²/g constant**, explicitly retained as an illustrative case already excluded at cluster scales; vSIDM (Correa+ 2021) is tuned to be consistent with massive-galaxy and cluster limits (L268-271). v19.2-F operates at **σ/m = 50-483 cm²/g at v ≈ 25-30 km/s**. No AIDA number in this paper was computed in that regime, so any σ/m value, core size or concentration offset taken from it to Cloud-9 is an extrapolation and must be labelled as one.
4. **Mass-range caveat:** AIDA's structural statements at the low-mass end rely on haloes resolved by ≥500 particles and it recommends its 51.7 Mpc boxes for M_vir ≲ 10¹⁰ M☉ (L510-511); Cloud-9 (M_200 ≈ 4.7×10⁹ M☉) sits at the edge of that, and the paper itself never quotes a concentration or core size at that mass. Do not invent one.

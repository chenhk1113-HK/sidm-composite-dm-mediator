## Key Findings

1. **Sample:** 15,000 velocity-jump candidates in Gaia DR3 across 80 streams (including GD-1's 4 official kicks and 103 candidate gaps).
2. **Complexity census (§4):** **75% of jumps have multiple/ambiguous origins** — two streams, nearby moving stars, binaries, or combinations; only **26% look like clean single encounters** with a perturber.
3. **GD-1 specifics (§5):** all four official GD-1 kicks I–IV are complex — contaminated by the nearby Orphan stream and/or a co-moving debris shell; the sharp GD-1 gap is better explained by a **bar-induced density wave** than a subhalo strike.
4. **Population fit (§6, Fig. 7):** single-power-law subhalo MMF with **α = -0.17 ± 0.03** and cutoff **M_enc < ~5×10⁶ M_sun**; α must steepen (α ≲ -0.4) to explain some observed kicks.
5. **Implication (their §7):** stream subhalo inference must model multi-stream and moving-contaminant populations — single-perturber fits overinterpret.

## How v19.2-F uses this paper

- **Adjudication (2026-11-03): CONTESTS — adopted as a METHODOLOGY CAVEAT on all GD-1 single-encounter evidence we cite.** Any statement of the form "GD-1 gap/spur X implies subhalo Y" now carries this caveat: 75% of candidate jumps are morphologically complex.

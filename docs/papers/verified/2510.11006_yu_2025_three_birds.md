## Why this entry exists

`v0.3-prelim/code/channels_extended.py` Channel 6 (gravitational-lensing substructure) used this
arXiv id under three different and mutually inconsistent names — "Yang, Yang, Yu et al. 2026",
"Yang+ GD-1 paper", and "Zhang+ 2025" — and presented `30-100 cm²/g` as a published preferred
range with a Gaussian likelihood built on it (finding F24). The GD-1 perturber paper is a *different*
work: arXiv:2409.19493, Zhang, Yu, Yang & Nadler, "The GD-1 stellar stream perturber as a
core-collapsed self-interacting dark matter halo" (submitted 2024-09-29).

## What the paper says (verbatim, `pdftotext -layout` line numbers)

- Lines 88-89 (figure caption): "…assuming SIDM cross sections per unit mass of σ/m = 0 cm²/g (CDM),
  30 cm²/g, 50 cm²/g, and 100 cm²/g."
- Lines 109-113: "From Figure 1, we find that the inferred density profiles of the B1938+666
  perturber, the Fornax substructure, and the GD-1 perturber are remarkably similar. All three are
  systematically denser and more compact in their inner regions than expected in the CDM framework,
  but they align closely with the profiles of core-collapsed SIDM halos. Despite residing in distinct
  environments, SIDM provides a unified explanation for their high densities, with a cross section of
  σ/m = 30-100 cm²/g."
- Line 217: "Ref. [9] assumed constant self-interaction cross sections …" (Ref. [9] is the prior
  GD-1/core-collapsed analysis, i.e. the 30-100 range is inherited as an assumption).
- Line 222: "…for σ/m ≳ 30 cm²/g (Eq. 2 of [9]), setting the lower [bound]" — a core-collapse
  timescale argument for a ~10⁸ M☉, V_max ≈ 10 km/s dwarf halo with concentration ≳ 2 above the
  cosmological median collapsing within a Hubble time.
- Line 224: "…amplitudes up to ≈100 cm²/g to induce core collapse" in more massive (sub)structures
  under velocity-dependent SIDM.

## What the paper does NOT publish

- No inferred cross section with a confidence interval for any of the three systems.
- No likelihood, posterior chain, or preferred range in the statistical sense; 30-100 cm²/g is the
  range the paper *adopts* and *argues is sufficient*, attributed to prior work.
- No statement that σ/m must be ≥ 30 cm²/g: the ≥30 figure is a sufficient-collapse condition for a
  specific halo class, not a lower bound on nature.

## Implication for v19.2-F

Channel 6's Gaussian (`LENS_SIGMA_M_LOG_PEAK = 1.7`, i.e. 50 cm²/g; `LENS_SIGMA_M_LOG_WIDTH = 0.7` dex)
is a **project-constructed prior** over the paper's adopted range, not a measurement. It is legitimate
as a modelling prior and must be labelled as such wherever it enters a channel tally or an
"observational constraint" claim. Numeric values were left unchanged on 2026-11-06; only the
attribution and status labels were corrected.

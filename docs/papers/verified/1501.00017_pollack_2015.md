## Thesis (from its own opening)

"We consider the cosmological consequences if a small fraction (f ≲ 0.1) of the dark matter is ultra-strongly self-interacting, with an elastic self-interaction cross-section per unit mass ≳ 1 cm²/g." Structure: §2 Supermassive Black Holes, §3 Gravothermal Collapse (§3A The Gravothermal Fluid Equations), §… M–σ relation discussion (line ~1008: "there exists a well-known (and relatively tight) relation between the properties of a host galaxy and the massive black hole it contains, the M–σ relation"). The mechanism is gravothermal catastrophe in the SIDM core producing a seed that becomes a supermassive black hole.

## Regime caveat before anyone transfers a number

Pollack+ study a **sub-component** of the dark matter (f ≲ 0.1) that is ultra-strongly self-interacting (≳ 1 cm²/g) in the early universe, aiming at high-redshift BH seeds. This project's Cloud-9 framework has the **whole** halo self-interacting at σ/m = 50-483 cm²/g today. The models are not the same object; cite [57] for the fluid calibration and the collapse mechanism only.

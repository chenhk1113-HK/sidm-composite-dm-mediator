## Key Findings

1. **Population inference** on the abundance of core-collapsed (sub)halos in the mass range 10^6 - 10^10.7 M_sun from the collective lensing signal (flux ratios) of 29 quadruply imaged quasars.
2. **Strong preference for core-collapsed halos:** Bayes factors disfavoring CDM relative to SIDM range **8:1 to 24:1**, depending on assumed SIDM subhalo abundance (ξ_sub) and the internal structure of deeply collapsed halos. Sellke calibration: Bayes factors 4.3 / 10 / 30 ≈ 2.0σ / 2.4σ / 2.9σ.
3. **Escape routes for CDM require extreme assumptions:** avoiding core collapse needs BOTH a globular-cluster abundance well above expectations AND more subhalos than N-body predicts.
4. **Collapse-timescale inference:** results expressed in two density-threshold collapse timescales, t6/8 and t8/10 (CDM defined as t > 80 Gyr; favored SIDM regions t < 5 Gyr in the illustrative cases). SIDM-favored lenses prefer higher subhalo abundance, shorter collapse times, higher projected GC mass density (e.g. J1042, RXJ1131; CDM-favored: H1413, PG1115).
5. **Systematics handled:** free rescaling ξ_sub for subhalo collapse accelerated (tidal stripping) or delayed (host heating) relative to field halos; baryons shift collapse time by only 10-20% at these halo masses; null-hypothesis calibration of the Bayes-factor test in Appendix D.

## How v19.2-F uses this paper

- **Adjudication (2026-11-03): ADOPT as population-level support for the core-collapse regime.** It does not measure σ/m at our velocity and does not constrain the resonant form; it supports the picture (core-collapsed subhalos are common where our framework puts them) at 8:1-24:1 against pure CDM.
- Candidate future comparison channel: our predicted collapsed-subhalo abundances vs their t6/8 / t8/10 posteriors — a clean testbed IF the project ever publishes a perturber-abundance prediction. Not started.

# arXiv:2605.05588 — Kitazawa 2026: SIDM and core formation in field LSB galaxies

**Status:** VERIFIED AS A CITATION (title/authors confirmed from the arXiv export API;
full PDF text read 2026-11-13). **Its published timescale numbers FAILED an
independent numerical cross-check and must NOT be quoted.** Adjudicated internally on
2026-11-13 without contacting the author (see "Adjudication without the author"); still
held in `to_verify/` only because it is unrefereed and a corrected version could appear.

**arXiv:** [2605.05588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05588) — submitted 2026-05-07
**Title:** "Self-interacting dark matter and core formation in field low-surface-brightness
galaxies"
**Author:** Noriaki Kitazawa (single author). No journal reference returned by the
arXiv export API as of 2026-11-13 (treat as unrefereed).

## What the paper claims

Five field LSB galaxies (F561-1, F563-V1, F571-V1, F574-1, F574-2) from the
Di Paolo, Salucci & Erkurt 2019 URC-LSBG IV sample (H I data: de Blok, McGaugh &
van der Hulst 1996) are tested for whether SIDM could have produced their cores.
Conclusion as printed: **sigma/m = 1 cm^2 g^-1 is excluded and sigma/m ~ 0.1 is
favoured**; the author states the estimate is order-of-magnitude only and rests on
the assumption that these LSBs decoupled from their proto-clusters at high z.

## Equations as printed (implemented verbatim)

```
t_r,0  = 1 / (a_h * rho_s * nu_s * sigma)          (paper eq. 11)
a_h    = sqrt(16 / pi) = 2.2568                    (hard-sphere factor, eq. 5)
nu_s   = sqrt(4 pi G rho_s r_s^2)                  (eq. 12)
t_core = 30 * t_r,0        "core has formed"       (eq. 7)
collapse / black-hole phase ~ 450 * t_r,0          (~15 t_core)
```

## Audit performed 2026-11-13 (this repo's reproduction)

`v0.3-prelim/code/channels_lsb_core.py::reproduce_kitazawa_table()` recomputes
t_core = 30 t_r,0 from the paper's own equations using the paper's own Table 1
parameters, with the SIDM venv (`.venv-sidm-bench`, numpy only):

| Galaxy | nu_s (km/s) | printed t_core @ sigma=1 | recomputed | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F561-1 | 61.2 | 0.54 Gyr | 565 Gyr | **1047** |
| F563-V1 | 38.5 | 0.87 Gyr | 899 Gyr | **1033** |
| F571-V1 | 108.5 | 0.30 Gyr | 319 Gyr | **1062** |
| F574-1 | 128.2 | 0.27 Gyr | 270 Gyr | **999** |
| F574-2 | 48.3 | 0.69 Gyr | 715 Gyr | **1037** |

Same ratio at sigma/m = 0.1 (ratio is velocity/cross-section independent, as it
must be). **The discrepancy is ~10^3 on 5/5 objects at both cross sections.**

Two checks establish where the error is NOT:
1. **The catalogue is fine.** An NFW mass integral `M(<R_c) = 4 pi rho_s r_s^3
   [ln(1+x) - x/(1+x)]` over the printed rho_s / r_s reproduces the printed
   M_dyn within 4% for all five galaxies (4.39e9 vs 4.57e9, 9.95e8 vs 1.00e9,
   1.24e10 vs 1.35e10, 2.79e10 vs 2.69e10, 3.13e9 vs 3.16e9). So
   `rho_s = 1.8e6 M_sun kpc^-3` really is per kpc^3, not per pc^3.
2. **The independent sanity anchor works.** Applied to a canonical dwarf
   (rho_s = 1e7 M_sun kpc^-3, nu_s = 100 km/s) the same formula gives
   t_r,0 = 2.1 Gyr at sigma/m = 1, i.e. collapse after a few relaxation times —
   the standard SIDM expectation. The formula is not the problem.

Consequence for the paper's conclusion: with the printed densities, the mean free
path at sigma/m = 1 is ~2.7 kpc / (1.2e-25 g cm^-3 x 1 cm^2 g^-1) ~ 2.7e3 kpc,
i.e. ~400 x the scale radius, so these LSBs are close to collisionless. Forming
their cores within ~11 Gyr would require **sigma/m >~ 25 – 80 cm^2 g^-1** at each
halo's own nu_s (computed by `sigma_min_for_core_formation()`), not 0.1. The
printed "sigma/m = 1 excluded, 0.1 favoured" conclusion does not follow from the
paper's own arithmetic.

Cross-check against this repo's own timescale (`v0.3-prelim/code/gravothermal.py`,
Balberg+ 2002 normalisation, `t_core = 12.7/sigma x (rho_s/1e7)^-1 x t_dyn`): for
the same five halos at sigma/m ~ 1 it gives t_core ~ 5 – 9 Gyr, i.e. between the
paper's printed values and the recomputation, and ~10^2 shorter than the strict
30-relaxation count. **Convention choice moves the LSB core-formation timescale by
2 – 3 orders of magnitude**, which is the deeper reason this half of the check
cannot pin sigma/m.

## Extended audit 2026-11-13 — all six printed tables, and publication status

Added while adjudicating whether this paper may be cited (`KITAZAWA_SCENARIOS`,
`reproduce_all_kitazawa_tables()`, `kitazawa_interpretation_audit()`).

**Publication status (checked 2026-11-13):** arXiv:2605.05588 has **v1 only**
(submitted 2026-05-07, 16 pages, single author), **no journal reference**, no
erratum, and INSPIRE reports **0 citations**. There is therefore no refereed or
corrected version to adjudicate against — the verdict below stands on this repo's
own reproduction. The author's own framing is "order-of-magnitude estimates
without numerical simulations".

The paper runs **three** proto-cluster initial conditions (its `A` column), each
with a sigma = 1 and a sigma = 0.1 table (Tables 2–7). Recomputing all six:

| Scenario | A | age used | entries | recomputed / printed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| z = 2.45 | 100 | 11 Gyr | 10 | 999 – 1062 |
| z = 7.66 | 10 | 13 Gyr | 10 | 1029 – 1072 |
| z = 7.88 | 5 | 13 Gyr | 10 | 952 – 1008 |

**30/30 entries land at ~10^3.** A uniform factor across three scenarios, three
densities (1.3e6 / 1.8e6 / 3.6e6), five scale radii (2.9 – 15 kpc) and both cross
sections is what a fixed unit slip looks like, not what scattered arithmetic errors
look like. The catalogue stays self-consistent in every scenario: `M(<R_c)/M_dyn`
spans **0.92 – 1.04** over all 15 rows.

### A second, independent defect: the paper's own thresholds are used inconsistently

`t_core = 30 t_r,0` is the paper's **core-formation** time; its collapse threshold
is ~450 t_r,0 = **15 t_core**. Applying both tests to the paper's *printed* numbers:

| Scenario | sigma/m | "t_core < age" | "15 t_core < age" |
|---|---|---|---|
| z = 2.45 | 1 | 5/5 | 4/5 |
| z = 2.45 | 0.1 | 5/5 | 0/5 |
| z = 7.66 | 1 | 5/5 | 3/5 |
| z = 7.66 | 0.1 | 5/5 | 0/5 |
| z = 7.88 | 1 | 5/5 | 5/5 |
| z = 7.88 | 0.1 | 5/5 | 0/5 |

Two things follow. (i) The test the table captions apply — "all values of t_core
are too small, indicating that black holes would form before the present time" —
is satisfied **5/5 in every one of the six tables**, for sigma/m = 1 *and* for
sigma/m = 0.1, so it cannot be what separates "1 excluded" from "0.1 favoured".
(ii) The discrimination the paper actually reports only appears at the **15 t_core**
scale (4/5, 3/5, 5/5 versus 0/5). Consistently with that, the paper's *prose* names
exceptions "except for F563-V1 and marginally F574-2" — precisely the two objects
the 15× test spares at z = 2.45 (13.1 Gyr and 10.4 Gyr against an 11 Gyr age) — while
its *captions* compare t_core itself to the age. The caption wording and the paper's
own definitions are not the same test.

## Adjudication without the author (2026-11-13) — RESOLVED as far as it can be

No enquiry was sent (owner's decision). The question was settled instead by
comparing **four** timescales for the same five halos at sigma/m = 1 cm^2 g^-1,
using this repo's published-formula implementations:

| Route | implementation | t_core at sigma/m = 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Paper's printed table | Table 2 | **0.27 – 0.87 Gyr** |
| Repo's Balberg+ 2002 convention | `gravothermal.py` (`12.7/sigma x (rho_s/1e7)^-1 x t_dyn`) | **7.0 Gyr** (identical for all five: same rho_s) |
| Paper's eq. (11)+(7) evaluated here | `channels_lsb_core.t_core_form_gyr` | **270 – 899 Gyr** |
| Yang+ 2024 eq. 2.2 (published 150C prefactor) | `gravothermal_yang2024.collapse_time_SI_gyr` | **2281 – 7605 Gyr** |
| Yang+ 2024 BM2 N-body calibrated | `collapse_time_calibrated_gyr` | **4151 – 13836 Gyr** |

Three findings resolve it:

1. **The paper's halo-to-halo pattern matches its own stated equations.** Our
   evaluation / printed ratio is 999–1062 over the five galaxies (and 952–1072 over
   all 30 printed entries), i.e. **one global factor of ~10^3 with ~5% scatter**
   (the scatter is the 2-significant-figure rounding of the printed values plus the
   paper's own rounded nu_s). Multiplying the printed column by 10^3 reproduces our
   numbers to 4–6% (540 vs 565, 870 vs 899, 300 vs 319, 270 vs 270, 690 vs 715). So
   eq. (11)–(12) were implemented correctly by the author; **the time column is
   quoted 10^3 below what those equations produce** (units of 10^3 Gyr labelled as
   Gyr), not a catalogue or arithmetic error.
2. **The scaling is corroborated by an independent published formula.** Yang+ 2024
   eq. 2.2 (`150 C / (sigma rho r sqrt(4 pi G rho))`) has the *identical* halo
   scaling: Yang/ours = **8.46 for all five halos** (a constant, as it must be —
   the two equations differ only in their dimensionless prefactor). Yang's own
   calibration halo BM2 (rho_s = 2.74e8 M_sun kpc^-3, r_s = 0.141 kpc, sigma/m =
   7.1) is reproduced by that function at 15.8 Gyr analytic / 28.7 Gyr N-body,
   matching the published value; the same code path on BM2 gives 1.86 Gyr. So the
   repo's evaluation is calibrated at the anchor halo and is not the source of a
   10^3 error.
3. **The honest verdict is a convention problem, not a single mistake.** The LSB
   core-formation timescale spans **0.3 -> 13,800 Gyr** across four published
   conventions for the same five halos — about 4.5 orders of magnitude, because the
   Balberg+ 2002 and Yang+ 2024 normalisations were both calibrated on *dense*
   halos (1e7 and 2.7e8 M_sun kpc^-3) and are being extrapolated to
   1.3e6 – 3.6e6 M_sun kpc^-3. Every one of them puts these LSBs far from collapse
   at sigma/m ~ 1 **except the paper's printed column**, so the printed claim
   "sigma/m = 1 excluded, 0.1 favoured" is the outlier and cannot be cited either
   way.

Corrected constraint from these five galaxies (our most conservative / shortest
times, `sigma_min_for_core_formation`): sigma/m >~ **24 – 82 cm^2 g^-1** for a core
to form within 11–13 Gyr, and >~ **12 – 41** at the paper's own collapse threshold
(15 t_core). Using Yang's calibrated times those floors rise by another factor of
~8. Either way **no constraint exists near sigma/m ~ 1**, which is the region the
paper claims to constrain.

**Production is unaffected:** the usable LSB half (Channel 14) uses
Sánchez-Salcedo+ 2005's own collapse time for NGC 5963 (`t_rc = 0.8 Gyr x
(sigma_0 / 1 cm^2 g^-1)^-1`, from their N-body collision model), not any of these
relaxation-count conventions.

## v19.2-F decision

- Implemented as **diagnostic only** (`kitazawa_lsb_rows()`,
  `reproduce_kitazawa_table()`); **not wired into any likelihood**.
- Do not cite 2605.05588 as evidence for sigma/m ~ 0.1.
- Do not cite it as an exclusion of sigma/m = 1 either, on the same grounds.
- **Citation policy (owner decision 2026-11-13): cite this paper as a cautionary
  audit only** — as an example of a published LSB core-formation timescale whose
  printed time column is 10^3 smaller than its own equations produce. Any citation
  must carry the correction (`sigma/m >~ 24-82 cm^2/g` for 11-13 Gyr core formation),
  never the paper's own 0.1 cm^2/g conclusion.
